ARTICLE 04

Standards of Appearance

Civilian attire is a privilege extended under MCO 1020.34H, and it can be withdrawn.

  • Marines at Innovation Tower may wear civilian clothing. However, the MCCUU in woodland MARPAT is required every Monday or the first workday following a liberty period, with grooming standards strictly enforced.
  • Marines must also comply with MCO 6110.3A W/CH-3, the Body Composition and Military Appearance Program. Failure to meet height and weight standards or maintain military appearance may result in assignment to the Body Composition Program or Military Appearance Program.
Administrative action

Any violation of uniform or grooming regulations will result in administrative action. The privilege of civilian attire can be revoked, with the potential consequence of requiring daily wear of the seasonal service uniform.

Civilian attire explorer

Three levels of dress are generally accepted across the DoD. Daily attire at MCSWF is Business Casual unless directed otherwise. Regardless of level, clothing must be clean, well-fitted, and in good condition — torn, faded, or excessively worn clothing is prohibited.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • When direct guidance is not available, use good situational awareness. Dress for the situation.
  • When TAD, pack extras — laundry service may not be available.
  • Remember you are a professional, in civilian attire or uniform.
  • Consider cultural norms when choosing attire.

Don't

  • Mistake civilian attire for “street” attire. It is not a pass to express your individuality.
  • Overdress. Even in civilian attire, you have a job to do.
  • Wear tank tops, crop tops, or low-cut or revealing tops.
  • Wear dresses or skirts above the knee — they must cover the knees at all times, walking, sitting, and standing.
  • Wear hoodies, unless Marine affiliated.
