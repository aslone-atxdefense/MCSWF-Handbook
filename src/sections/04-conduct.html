ARTICLE 03

Code of Conduct

Marines selected to serve at MCSWF are expected to demonstrate professional and mature behavior at all times, on and off duty.

Marines are required to maintain law-abiding conduct in accordance with local, state, and federal laws, as well as the UCMJ, DoD regulations, Marine Corps policies, and MCSWF guidelines.

All Marines are expected to:

  • Uphold the standards of this handbook, which provides guidance for appropriate actions in all situations.
  • Take responsibility for personal conduct, as it contributes to the overall discipline and order of the unit.
  • Communicate respectfully, addressing others appropriately by rank and name. Civilian and contractor personnel are addressed as “Sir” or “Ma'am” unless directed otherwise.
  • Be punctual and prepared for all meetings, in person or virtual. Cameras on during virtual interactions to enhance communication and emotional intelligence, unless circumstances such as bandwidth dictate otherwise.

Government vs. contractor personnel

You will work alongside government civilians and contractors every day. The line between the two is legal, not social — and crossing it creates real liability. These guidelines come from the Defense Acquisition University's Contracting Officer's Representative training.

Civilian / GS

The government obtains its employees by direct hire under competitive appointment or other civil service procedures. Government personnel are hired, fired, supervised, and rewarded by the government.

Contractor / CTR

Contractor personnel are employees of a contractor, subject to their individual contracts with their employer and to their employer's contract with the government — not to your direction.

Inherently governmental functions

A contractor is prohibited from performing specific functions that are inherently governmental FAR 7.503. Only government officials can legally perform these, because they are so intimately related to the public interest:

  • Direction and control of federal employees
  • Determination of budget policy, guidance, and strategy
  • Resource allocation or program management duties
  • Approval of contractual documents or administering contracts
  • Obligating congressionally authorized funding
